Every 2 milliseconds, one packet each way

Webots computes the physics; ArduPilot computes the motor commands. Neither may run ahead of the other. The world sets the physics step to 2 ms, and the bridge turns every step into a handshake: one sensor packet out, one motor packet back, one step.

Two programs share the job of flying. Webots works out the physics: where the drone goes when the motors push. ArduPilot works out the motor commands from what the drone senses. 6.1

From a PWM number to lift

ArduPilot computes each motor's output as a PWM width in microseconds; the SITL backend sends (pwm − 1000) / 1000. Webots' propellers produce thrust T = k·|ω|·ω, quadratic in rotor speed, while ArduPilot with MOT_THST_EXPO 0 assumes thrust linear in its output. Setting ω = √u · ωmax makes T = k·ωmax²·u: linear again.

Each motor's command from ArduPilot is a pulse width (PWM) between 1000 and 2000 microseconds, sent to Webots as (pwm − 1000) / 1000, a number from 0 to 1. ArduPilot assumes thrust grows in proportion to that number. But a propeller's thrust grows with the square of its speed, T = k·|ω|·ω. Setting the speed to the square root of the command, ω = √u · ωmax, cancels the square: T = k·ωmax²·u, proportional again.

That gives a check you can do by hand. With k = 0.0012 and ωmax = 100 rad/s from the drone's proto, four motors give 48·u newtons. Hovering (48–52 s), this run's motors averaged 1305 µs, so u ≈ 0.305 and thrust ≈ 14.6 N, within half a percent of the weight of the proto's mass 1.5 kg (14.7 N).
